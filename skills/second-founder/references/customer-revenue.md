# Customer and revenue decisions

Use during onboarding, weekly prioritization, or diagnosis of weak acquisition, payment or retention. Carry one coherent buyer hypothesis from research through the product journey to observed paid outcomes. Reuse the project's profile, `facts.md`, aggregate adapter, experiments and action ledger; do not create a second customer database.

This module does not expand authority. The starting strategy keeps the U.S. and UK first in acquisition work, includes useful opportunities elsewhere, targets two quality-gated new blogs per day and permits no additional spending; honor explicit saved owner overrides. Publishing and tested reversible improvements require the current owner's saved scope. Pricing, billing, destructive actions, outreach and customer messaging require their applicable separate authority. All creative work follows the saved content policy; the `modest_music_free` preset allows narration and prohibits music.

## Establish the buyer and buying situation

Save a compact commercial model, with evidence dates and unknowns, before treating a broad audience as a validated market:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Buyer segment | A specific role/use case and situation the shipped product can serve; identify the user, payer and decision maker when they differ |
| Purchase trigger | The task, deadline, failure or change that makes solving the problem worth acting on now |
| Current alternative | The actual competing product, manual workflow, internal process or decision to do nothing |
| Value and fit | The first useful result, relevant existing paid benefit, meaningful limitations and who is a poor fit |
| Buying objections | The few evidenced reasons to hesitate, with sources and the proof needed to resolve them |
| Reach and intent | Where this segment already seeks help or evaluates solutions; distinguish research interest from a buying signal |
| Commercial path | Current offer, actual purchase motion, payer/account/order unit, conversion milestones and a supported time-to-outcome window |

Use actual product capability, existing aggregate customer/support evidence, public buyer questions and current competitor facts. Label assumptions. A rival's feature or a popular keyword alone does not establish demand for this product. Avoid inventing a persona, budget or willingness to pay from geography.

Start with the strongest supported segment and its buying situation; retain other credible segments in the queue. Separate materially different buyers when their purchase path or objections differ. Review the priority when paid outcomes or repeated fit failures contradict it. Do not fragment tiny samples into apparently precise country-by-persona conversion rates.

## Map the actual route to payment

Use the business's existing route, not every row as a requirement:

| Motion | Example stages to map to actual evidence | Essential distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Self-service | Relevant visit → signup if required → first value → existing paid benefit/offer → checkout → confirmed payment | Activation is useful product value, not payment; an upgrade click is not a customer |
| Sales-assisted | Relevant inquiry → qualified opportunity → existing evaluation/proposal stages → confirmed payment | Define fit/qualification and stage timestamps; pipeline value and signed intent are not receipts |
| Transactional | Relevant product/service visit → offer/order intent → checkout or existing invoice path → confirmed payment → delivery/value | Payment can precede first value; repeat purchase may be meaningful, optional or inapplicable |

Hybrid products can have more than one path. State the entry event, eligible denominator and expected delay for each. Use existing aggregate CRM/admin reports when available; reading opportunities does not authorize contacting them. Keep raw lead/customer records in their authorized systems.

Call bot-filtered traffic **eligible visits**. Call demand qualified only when recorded fit or buying-intent criteria support it; otherwise keep it unknown. Helpful indicators can include a relevant use-case inquiry or progression through an existing buying flow, but no single pageview proves qualification.

Trace payment outcomes to the supported revenue source, using its actual success statuses and refund treatment. A client-side purchase event, checkout return URL, created subscription or invoice alone does not prove collected payment. Reconcile aggregate counts where possible, record mismatches, and preserve unknown attribution.

## Reach the first paying customer

Determine the state from verified evidence:

- **Verified zero paid:** the bound payment/admin evidence and documented history are sufficient to establish no qualifying successful payments to date. Record coverage and any legacy payment path. Zero payments in a recent reporting window alone does not establish a first-customer business.
- **Observed paid:** successful payments exist; record the covered period, existing baseline and earliest known payment without pretending partial history is complete.
- **Unknown:** access, history, status definitions or reconciliation is insufficient, including a zero-payment window with unknown earlier history. Repair the evidence source while continuing independently useful work; do not label this a zero-customer business.

For verified zero paid, choose one supported segment/problem and the next unresolved milestone toward payment:

1. Check the existing offer's relevance and show a real useful result through a working demo, sample or template.
2. Provide one clear next action on a relevant landing page and exercise the actual purchase journey through supported previews/test facilities. Do not make a real purchase to validate it.
3. Put the verified offer in front of the relevant audience through authorized mapped channels and buyer-intent content. Prepare other channel opportunities when submission or contact authority is missing.
4. Inspect available aggregate intent, fit, activation and purchase-friction evidence. Use existing questions/support themes; new interviews, DMs, invitations or email need the relevant messaging authority.
5. Fix the first supported obstacle, verify the change, and review at the milestone's appropriate delay. Record what was learned even if no sale occurs.

Keep the two-blog quality target, but do not confuse publishing completion with commercial validation. Align useful articles and distribution with the selected buyer problem and current obstacle. No deadline, post count or manual effort guarantees a first customer.

## Diagnose activation to payment

For each relevant path, map first value, encounter with the existing paid benefit, checkout/order intent and confirmed payment to real observable events. The sequence can differ by business; do not force a free trial or invent an upgrade screen.

Compare cohorts with enough time to reach the outcome, using aligned entry dates, eligibility, acquisition source and known segment where coverage permits. Distinguish user activation from an account's payment and exclude internal/test activity. Separate unavailable events from a measured drop-off; add only necessary scoped measurement through the verified deployment path.

| Observed obstacle | Bounded next action |
|---|---|
| Relevant visitors cannot understand fit | Clarify the supported use case, limitations and next step on its landing page |
| Eligible signups do not reach first value | Repair a reproducible failure or improve a demonstrated onboarding step/template |
| Activated users cannot find or understand an existing paid benefit | Show the relevant shipped benefit and accurate current plan explanation at the appropriate moment |
| Buying intent reaches a broken or confusing handoff | Repair the verified navigation/integration defect or clarify the existing process within scope |
| Checkout starts but confirmed payments lag | Inspect supported aggregate errors/statuses and maturity; distinguish technical failure, delay, fit and unknown causes |

Do not infer that every nonpayer needs a discount or that more prompts will create demand. Price changes, trial terms, billing configuration and customer charges remain outside default authority. Keep the purchase decision voluntary and the current offer accurately represented.

## Resolve objections with evidence buyers can find

Maintain a short proof queue: buyer objection; affected journey/page; evidence already available; missing proof; smallest useful improvement; verification and review date. Work on objections supported by actual evidence, not a universal trust-badge checklist.

Useful proof includes a working product demonstration, representative sample output, current capability/limit documentation, compatibility steps, a reachable existing support route, or an applicable published policy. Use a permissioned customer result only when both its claims and publication rights are established. Label worked examples; never invent endorsements, security assurances or legal promises.

Put the proof near the relevant decision and verify the live path. Content elsewhere on the site does not establish that a buyer can find it during evaluation or checkout. Preserve factual qualifications and show meaningful limitations.

Check U.S. and UK buyer readiness against actual product support: terminology, documented availability, existing displayed price/currency and billing period, usable forms, time-zone expectations and supported integrations. State constraints clearly. Do not silently change price, tax, currency, availability, legal terms or access, and do not duplicate country pages without distinct value. Record real conflicts or propose broader changes while continuing supported work for other buyers.

## Keep paying customers and identify lifecycle opportunities

Product return visits and payer retention answer different questions. Establish the business-appropriate eligible cohort and interval:

- Subscriptions: retained/renewed paying accounts, cancellations and retained revenue, with expansion/contraction separated where available. Separate requested cancellation from its effective date and voluntary loss from known payment failures.
- Transactions: refunds, delivery/value failures and repeat paid purchases when repeat demand is natural. Do not mark a satisfied one-time buyer as churn merely because another order was unnecessary.
- Both: inspect aggregate support, reliability and fit themes alongside outcomes; record unknown reasons rather than assigning intent to every cancellation or refund.

Connect a supported loss pattern to a reversible remedy: clarify expectations, repair a recurring failure, improve a useful repeat workflow, or make existing help easier to find. Do not hide cancellation/refund routes, modify terms, refund charges or change subscriptions under growth authority.

Look for onboarding, unfinished evaluation, renewal guidance, return-to-value, referral or win-back opportunities only when relevant to the actual product. Existing in-product documentation and tested guidance can be improved within saved scope. Customer email, DM, invitation, referral-request and lifecycle campaigns remain concrete proposals unless separately authorized for the audience, channel and action. A connected messaging tool, existing address or customer relationship does not supply that authority. Do not send test messages to real customers.

For a proposal, prepare its evidence, audience definition, useful message/value, destination/system, resource cost, success measure and stopping rule without copying personal customer data into the proposal. Continue authorized product or content improvements while the proposed campaign is unavailable.

## Learn with the evidence the project actually has

Verify the relevant events/source before optimizing their conversion rates. Record aggregate counts alongside rates, reporting delay, cohort maturity and missing coverage. Do not compare recent signups with mature paying cohorts or treat a small count difference as a proven winner.

For low traffic, choose a bounded learning task supported by observable evidence: reproduce a journey failure, evaluate an existing buyer objection, inspect aggregate support themes, or test comprehension using available authorized feedback. Qualitative findings can justify a reversible improvement, but they do not establish population-wide uplift.

Use A/B evaluation only when the actual allocation, event quality, sample and observation window support a meaningful decision. Define the decision rule before reading results and retain inconclusive outcomes; daily peeking or a calendar deadline does not create statistical certainty. A before/after observation remains directional when confounding changes cannot be excluded.

Track payment and customer-value outcomes as they mature. Select guardrails relevant to the change, such as refunds, complaints, failed core journeys, cancellations or existing operating cost. A signup increase that brings poor-fit users or damages paid retention is not automatically a win.

## Make one weekly capacity decision

Reuse the current weekly decision in daily runs and check its next due date; do not reconstruct it merely to complete a daily checklist. If no decision exists, save a provisional choice from available evidence. Reconsider early when an incident or material new customer evidence changes the bottleneck, recording why.

Compare the current acquisition, activation-to-paid, qualified-pipeline and paid-retention obstacles using available evidence. Preserve urgent reliability work and required due operations. Then choose the highest-value bounded commercial improvement or learning task that fits the remaining included capacity.

Record: selected buyer/outcome; evidence and uncertainty; expected mechanism; effort/allowance estimate; next review date; and the best alternative being deferred. Avoid fabricated ROI scores. A serious loss of existing customer value can outrank acquisition, and weak reach can outrank another product feature.

Use the decision to update the rolling 30-day plan and existing experiment queue. Keep the two-blog quality target, but concentrate content, proof and distribution around useful buyer problems. Log honest shortfalls when quality, incidents or capacity prevent completion; never add spending, filler or catch-up bursts to satisfy the count.

Review mature outcomes, stop unsupported repetition, and preserve lessons. If the highest-value action needs broader authority, prepare the concrete reviewable proposal and execute the next useful authorized task. Report measured business results separately from shipped work and hypotheses.
